About

Every day, each of us picks the shop that does it better, the faster phone, the cheaper flight. Add technology, which does more of our work every year at a lower cost. If billions of people keep choosing more for less, prices should fall. That is the natural state of a free market. Your money should buy more every year, even if you did nothing.

So why do prices rise?

Because our money is built on debt, and debt can’t survive falling prices. More money has to be created every year to keep it alive. That takes value from your savings and your time without asking you. And a system that takes without asking needs more control every year to keep going.

It’s easy to blame bankers or politicians. Change them and you’d get the same result. The system gets its strength from us. We feed it every time we try to get ahead inside it.

Bitcoin changes one thing. Nobody can create more of it. As long as it stays decentralized and secure, it gives you a way out: a place to put your time and energy where the gains of technology flow back to you. The door of the cell is open.

But you can buy bitcoin and stay in the cell. You show a company your ID, leave your coins with it, and take the interest it offers. It feels safe. Mt. Gox, Celsius and FTX felt safe too. Each took in its customers’ coins, and each went bankrupt. Bitcoin has almost one law: don’t try to cheat it. The people who try are the ones who get wiped out.

The world doesn’t just happen to you. It is the sum of what all of us do, and you have a say. Where you buy bitcoin is part of that say. Buoy was made to show which way each choice points.

What Buoy does

Buoy compares companies that sell bitcoin and nothing else. Pick two, and it puts them side by side on five questions:

  • Privacy. Can you buy without showing ID, and pay without your bank?
  • Self-custody. Do the coins go straight to you, with no balance left at the company?
  • Medium of exchange. Does it deliver over Lightning, so you can spend what you buy?
  • Fiat incentives. Does it stay away from loans, yield, and balances in fiat or stablecoins?
  • Verify. Is the software open source, so anyone can check it?

A yes points toward a free market. A no points toward control. Most companies have some of each, and Buoy shows that too.

Buoy doesn’t rank the companies or tell you where to buy. It doesn’t compare fees. Other sites do that well. It doesn’t call a company good or bad. It shows the choices each company has made, taken from what the company publishes itself, so you can see what your own choice adds to.

Who made it

I’m Jasper van de Ree, a designer based in Amsterdam. The argument at the top of this page is Jeff Booth’s, in my words. I first heard him in a podcast, saying that the natural state of a free market is deflation. It made sense at once. Then I read his book, The Price of Tomorrow, and I couldn’t look at money the old way again.

But I was still buying my bitcoin through an app that sells other crypto currencies. Then I used an app that was Bitcoin-only but had my ID and held my coins. When I looked for something more aligned with Bitcoin, the number of companies overwhelmed me. In time I noticed I wasn’t choosing by fees or features. I was choosing by the founders: does what they’re building lead out of the old system, or back into it? Bitcoin’s short history is full of leaders who brought the old habits with them, and of the rest of us, who followed them for a better rate.

Buoy is how I put that question in order. I made it for the person I was then. Each of us has to make this journey ourselves, and Buoy is my small contribution to yours.
